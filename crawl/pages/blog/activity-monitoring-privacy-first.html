Activity monitoring vs. surveillance: building privacy-first desktop tracking

February 19, 2026
5 min read
BetterFlow Team

Employee monitoring software has a reputation problem. Stories of companies tracking keystrokes, taking screenshots every five minutes, and logging every website visit have made workers suspicious of any time tracking tool. But there is a meaningful difference between surveillance (watching employees to catch wrongdoing) and activity monitoring (helping employees understand how they spend their time).

At BetterQA, we built BetterFlow's activity monitoring to be open about what it records. Here is how architecture decisions shape whether a tool helps or harms.

What data is actually needed?

Most surveillance tools collect far more data than necessary. Screenshots, keystrokes, and webcam feeds are not needed to answer the question "how much time did I spend on this project today?"

BetterFlow's desktop agent collects:

  • Active application name: Which app is in the foreground
  • Window title: What document or tab is active, stored as recorded
  • Duration: How long each app was active
  • Idle detection: Periods of no keyboard/mouse activity
  • Input counts: How many keystrokes, clicks and scrolls there were, as numbers only
  • Web domain: The domain of the active page, such as github.com, not the full address by default
  • Hardware serial number: Identifies the computer so IT can match it to the company asset list

What it does NOT collect:

  • Screenshots
  • Which keys you press or what you type
  • Clipboard contents
  • File contents
  • Browser history (only the domain of the active tab is kept by default, not the full address)
  • Webcam or microphone data

What employees can see and do

Surveillance assumes the employer owns all data about employee activity. We try to give employees some say in how their time data is used:

A notice in the desktop app: The desktop app shows a notice listing what it records and giving a contact for questions, and keeps showing it until the employee acknowledges it. Employees can also see their own activity and window titles in BetterFlow.

Control over tracked time: Employees can assign tracked time to a project, mark it as not worked, or delete it. Which apps count as development, social or entertainment is set by BetterQA administrators, and time can end up labelled as a distraction.

Opt-out for sensitive work: Employees can pause tracking during personal activities, confidential meetings, or simply when they want privacy.

Window titles: what is stored and who can see it

Window titles often contain sensitive information: "Performance Review - John Smith.docx" or "Salary Planning Q3.xlsx". A title can also show an email subject, a page title or a terminal tab name. BetterFlow's desktop agent sends window titles as they are, and they are stored as recorded, in readable form, on BetterFlow's servers.

Employees can see their own titles in the app. Company admins can see the titles of everyone in their company, and BetterQA's support administrators can see them too. To write short activity summaries, titles are sent to an AI service (Google Gemini) after email addresses and long numbers are removed where possible. Names are not removed.

Before rollout, tell your team what is collected, who can read it, and why. The agent does not record which keys you press, the text you type, the body of messages or screenshots. On macOS it skips 1Password, Keychain Access and System Settings by default. Web addresses are kept to the domain by default, though the page title is part of the window title.

What company admins can see

How data is reported matters as much as what is collected. Surveillance-oriented tools show managers exactly what each employee did minute by minute. BetterFlow's reporting is not only aggregate, so here is what a company admin can see:

  • A daily timeline for each employee, listing the apps used, how long each was active, and the window titles behind each entry
  • Time grouped into core tasks, personal tasks and distractions, with short written summaries of each stretch of work. Time can be labelled as a distraction

A company admin can therefore see more than "Sarah averaged 6.5 productive hours per day this month". They can open a day and see that Sarah spent 47 minutes in one app, and what its window titles said. Decide with your team who should use that view and how often, and tell employees before rollout.

Legal and ethical considerations

GDPR and similar regulations require legitimate business purpose for data collection. "We want to spy on employees" is not legitimate. "We need accurate project costing" is legitimate, but it does not justify collecting screenshots.

The principle of data minimization requires collecting only what is necessary. For time tracking, that starts with app names and durations. Window titles, input counts and a hardware serial number go beyond that, so be ready to explain to your team why each is collected. The serial number, for example, is there so IT can match a computer to the company asset list. Keystroke logs are not needed, and BetterFlow does not keep them.

Building trust through transparency

When implementing activity monitoring, transparency builds trust:

  1. Explain the purpose: Why are we collecting this data? (Accurate project costing, capacity planning)
  2. Show what is collected: Let employees see their own raw data
  3. Demonstrate value: Use the data to help employees, not just monitor them
  4. Give control: Let employees pause tracking and manage their own tracked time

When employees see activity monitoring as a tool that helps them (track their own productivity, justify their hours to clients, identify where time goes) rather than a surveillance tool, adoption improves dramatically.
