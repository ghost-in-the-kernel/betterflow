Top 10 time tracking apps with AI features in 2026

January 08, 2026
6 min read
BetterFlow Team

Time tracking has moved on from the start and stop timer. Many tools now watch which apps and sites you used, suggest entries for you, or check what you wrote against other records. This guide compares ten of them by what they do, so you can match one to how your team works.

Disclosure: BetterFlow is built by BetterQA, the company that publishes this blog, and it is on the list. The tools below are in alphabetical order, not ranked. We say where BetterFlow is not the right choice.

How to choose

Five questions sort most of these tools:

  • How does time get in? A timer, automatic capture from apps and sites, or a draft that you edit.
  • Is it checked against anything? Most tools trust what people type. A few compare it with commits, tickets or calendars.
  • How is it priced? Per person, or one price for the company. Per-person pricing grows with headcount.
  • How much does it watch? Screenshots and activity scores suit some teams and cause friction in others.
  • Who sees what? Check where data is stored and who can view one person's activity.

BetterFlow

Best for: software and services teams that bill clients by the hour and want entries they can defend.

BetterFlow charges one flat price for the whole company instead of a price per person, so cost does not rise as the team grows (the current figure is on the pricing page). It scores every entry for detail, and when a project is connected to GitHub it compares the entry with that day's commits, pull requests and reviews. Jira ticket context can be pulled into a description. An optional desktop agent fills in the day automatically, and leave management is built in.

You can read how the scoring works on our page about AI timesheets.

Trade-off: the cross-check needs something to compare against, so it adds little if your team does not use GitHub. It also has fewer third-party integrations than the large, established tools.

Clockify

Best for: teams that want a low-cost start.

Clockify offers timers, timesheets and reports, with a generous free tier and an optional auto-tracker. It also suggests entries from patterns in your history.

Trade-off: entries are mostly checked by a person reading them, and some advanced features sit on paid plans.

Hubstaff

Best for: remote or field teams paid by tracked activity.

Hubstaff measures activity across applications and can capture screenshots, and it covers payroll and team analytics.

Trade-off: screenshots and activity scores are monitoring features. Some teams value the accountability and others find it intrusive, so decide that before you buy.

Memory

Best for: individuals who want to reconstruct what they worked on.

Memory keeps a searchable record of the applications, documents and pages you used, so you can ask what you did on a given afternoon. It is a personal work memory more than a team timesheet.

Trade-off: it is not built for team approval, billing or client reports.

Qbserve

Best for: Mac users who want data to stay on their own machine.

Qbserve tracks application use on a Mac and sorts it into productive, neutral and distracting. Data is kept locally.

Trade-off: it is Mac-focused and aimed at individuals more than teams.

RescueTime

Best for: people who want to improve their own focus.

RescueTime tracks time in applications and websites, scores it as productive or distracting, and helps you set focus goals.

Trade-off: it measures attention rather than billable work, so it is a weak fit for client timesheets.

Rize

Best for: individuals who want tracking with coaching on focus and breaks.

Rize tracks work automatically and adds focus sessions and break reminders based on your own patterns.

Trade-off: like RescueTime, it centres on personal productivity more than team billing.

Time Doctor

Best for: teams that want distraction alerts and detailed monitoring.

Time Doctor flags time on non-work sites, reports on workload across the team and can capture screenshots.

Trade-off: it is a monitoring tool. Expect a conversation with your team about how it is used.

Timely

Best for: people who want entries drafted for them without giving up privacy.

Timely records your work activity in a private timeline and suggests entries from application use, documents and meetings. Individual detail stays private, and team leads see summaries.

Trade-off: you still need to review and approve what it suggests.

Toggl Track

Best for: teams that want a simple timer with many integrations.

Toggl Track combines a quick timer with a timeline of your activity, and it suggests entries from calendar events and past patterns. It connects to a long list of other tools.

Trade-off: it records what people say they did and does not check it against other records.

Which one fits

  • You bill clients by the hour and need entries that stand up to questions: look at BetterFlow, or any tool with a real cross-check.
  • You want the cheapest start: try Clockify.
  • You care most about your own focus, not team billing: RescueTime or Rize.
  • You are comfortable with monitoring and screenshots: Hubstaff or Time Doctor.

Whatever you pick, run a two-week trial with a few real projects before you move the whole team. Features and prices change, so check each vendor's current pages.
